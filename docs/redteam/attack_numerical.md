# Red-team review — Numerical / Counting facet

**Date:** 12 Jul 2026
**Scope:** counting pipeline (`toolbox.counting` / `heads/numerical.py`), GT-battery count scoring (`groundtruth/scoring.py`), the 15–27% independent-agreement result in `reports/gt_battery_full_2026-07-11/`, and whether the planned cvsweep rerun optimizes the right counting objective.
**Method:** code reading plus two targeted probes over the real VLA-3D data (`data/vla3d/Unity/`, main-repo checkout): all 15 numerical questions re-run with the `resolve()` audit trail exposed, per-instance predicate explanations dumped for every disagreeing scene, and label-match pools inspected.

## Verdict

The measured 15–27% independent count agreement is **not** a perception problem and mostly **not** an annotation-semantics problem — it is dominated by counting-path logic defects that the probes reproduce deterministically. Blame apportionment for the *measured* disagreement: **(a) annotation/scorer-semantics mismatch ≈ 35%** — the "independent" reference counts are themselves inflated by substring/token class matching (livingroom_3: indep=10 distinct "photo" targets in a scene with 9 photo objects), deflated by annotation coverage gaps (chinese_room: indep=1 vs scene-graph=6=pipeline; japanese_room/studio: **zero** annotated targets of the queried class), and the `*_class_only` columns are relation-agnostic totals compared against relation-filtered counts (arabic_room "1 vs 3" is a scorer artifact, the pipeline count is plausibly correct). **(b) fusion/dedup/tracker defects = 0% of the measured signal** — the GT battery builds the index directly from the object CSV (`gt_battery.py:234`, `BasicSceneIndex(gt.instances)`; the constructor never calls the merge path), so `fusion.py`, `tracker.py`, and the `scene_index` merge machinery were never executed in this measurement; they are *unexonerated*, not innocent (see F8). **(c) counting-gate/resolution logic ≈ 65%** — three concrete, probe-confirmed defects: `counting()` inherits `resolve()`'s answer-something fallback ladder and returns the whole-category count when relation/attribute filters empty (fired on **6 of 15** questions, e.g. home_building_1 answers **31**); typo-tolerant label lookup matches colour *aliases* ("yellow" is edit-distance 2 from "pillow", adding 13 phantom pillows in home_building_1); and the `on`/`above` predicate geometry structurally fails on VLA-3D furniture boxes (0 of 25 pillows across three scenes pass `on(sofa/bed)` because sofa/bed AABB tops are backrest/headboard heights). Consequence for the sweep: **do not rerun cvsweep for the counting term as-is** — its numerical objective targets the artifact-laden independent counts (including class-only ones), its `on_vert_tol` range (≤0.30) cannot reach the real −0.20…−1.08 m gaps so the objective is flat exactly where counting breaks, and `counting.min_obs` is provably inert on GT data. Fix F1–F4/F6 first (mostly S-cost), then sweep.

Points context: numerical is 1 pt/scene × 3 hidden scenes = **3 of 51 eval points (~5.9%)**. On the training set, current code gets an estimated 7–9 of 15 numericals wrong against best-guess truth, i.e. an expected eval loss of roughly **1.4–1.8 of the 3 pts**. Fixes below are cheap relative to that, but priority stays below IF/OR per `docs/question_analysis.md`.

---

## F1 — `counting()` reuses `resolve()`'s fallback ladder: an empty filter answers "all instances of the noun", not 0

- **Severity:** ~1.0–1.5 pts (of the 3 numerical eval pts). This is the primary over-count. The fallback fired on 6/15 training numericals (home_building_1 → 31, home_building_2 → 10, loft → 11, hotel_room_2 → 5, hotel_room_1 → 4, studio → 3, livingroom_4 → 7 incl. F3 pollution). Two of those were *coincidentally* right (hotel_room_1: all 4 pillows in the room happen to be on the bed; studio unknown) — the failure is masked whenever category-total == true count, which will not hold in hidden scenes. 13/15 training numericals are in the on/above/with family this affects.
- **Evidence:** `src/core/geometry/toolbox.py:557` (`counting` calls `resolve`), `toolbox.py:460-490` (fallback ladder: `relax_attributes` → `drop_relation` → `category_only` returns `list(pool) or list(base)`), and `counting` at `toolbox.py:545-559` ignores `res.audit` entirely. Probe (all-15 run): `home_building_1 "How many pillows are on the sofa under the pictures?" COUNT=31, audit=[('category_only', "fell back to all 'pillow' instances")]`; `home_building_2 "How many red pillows are on the sofa?" COUNT=10, audit=[('relax_attributes', "dropped ['red']"), ('category_only', ...)]`. Report rows match (`reports/gt_battery_full_2026-07-11/gt_battery_report.md:30,35,75`).
- **Attack scenario:** any hidden-scene question "How many X are on/above the Y?" where the relation filter yields zero under our predicates (which, per F2, is the *normal* case for pillows/pictures): we publish the total X count of the scene. A house-scale scene ("home_building"-class) makes the error unbounded (31 vs a true answer of ~2–5).
- **Proposed fix (design):** the fallback ladder is correct for object-reference (must return *something* to box) and wrong for counting (cardinality of a filtered set). `counting()` should run the filter pipeline *without* relaxation rungs: noun+attributes+clauses, and return 0 with the failed-clause explanation when empty. If a "relation unmeasurable vs. genuinely zero" distinction is wanted, return the audit to the head and let the FSM watchdog fall back to the modal-integer floor instead of the category total. Never count a `relax_attributes` or `category_only` result.
- **Cost:** S (one function + head plumbing; the audit trail already exists).

## F2 — `on`/`above` predicate geometry structurally mismatches VLA-3D furniture boxes; fixing F1 alone converts the over-count into a 0-under-count

- **Severity:** shares the F1 pool (~1.0–1.5 pts): both must land to collect. With F1 fixed naively, "How many pillows are on the bed?" answers **0** (true: 4) — exact-match scoring gives the same 0 pts as answering 31.
- **Evidence:** `toolbox.py:159-178`: `on()` requires `|a.bottom − b.top| ≤ 0.15 m`. VLA-3D sofa/bed AABBs top out at backrest/headboard height, so a pillow on the *seat* can never pass. Probe: hotel_room_1, all 4 pillows: `bottom 0.66 vs top 1.74 (vgap −1.08m ... FAIL); footprint overlap 100% ok`. loft, all 11 pillows fail (vgap −0.35…−0.58 m); home_building_2, all 10 fail (−0.20…−0.27 m). `toolbox.py:244-255`: `above()` requires XY footprint overlap; hotel_room_2, all 5 wall-hung pictures fail exactly there (`footprint overlap FAIL; vertical gap +0.53m ok`) though 3 are annotated above the bed. Zero passes in 30 real support/above relations probed.
- **Attack scenario:** every "pillows on the sofa/bed" and "pictures above the bed" question — the most common numerical template (8 of 15 training numericals involve pillows or pictures).
- **Proposed fix (design):** redefine support: `on(a,b)` = footprint-overlap ≥ frac AND `a.bottom` within `[b.zmin + margin, b.ztop + tol]` (resting on *any* horizontal surface of b, not only its top plane) — or equivalently `b.zmin < a.bottom ≤ b.ztop + tol`. Redefine `above(a,b)` with a laterally inflated footprint test (pad by ~0.5 m or by b's half-width) so wall-mounted objects above headboards qualify. Calibrate against the VLA-3D scene-graph "On"/"Above" edges (the generator's own thresholds are in `docs/prior_art/vla_3d.md`) — that is the *right* use of the annotations: fitting the relation definition, not the per-question counts.
- **Cost:** M (predicate redesign + recalibration + object-ref regression check, since `on` also feeds OR/IF anchor resolution).

## F3 — typo-tolerant lookup matches colour aliases and 3-letter labels: "yellow" ≡ "pillow", "tap" ≡ "cup", "bag" ≡ "bed"

- **Severity:** ~0.3–0.5 pts. Adds phantom instances to both target pools and anchor pools. home_building_1: +13 non-pillows counted as pillows (bottles, cookers, a dining table — every one via a "yellow" dominant-colour alias at Levenshtein distance 2). livingroom_4: the answer is off by exactly this (+1 "mirror", aliases `('brown','yellow','black')`); livingroom_2 cup pool includes a "tap"; hotel_room_2's *bed anchor set* includes a "bag" (existential any-anchor then evaluates relations against the bag).
- **Evidence:** `src/core/perception/scene_index.py:149-153` (`by_label` typo tier runs edit-distance ≤ 2 over `[canon] + normalize_label(aliases)`), `src/core/groundtruth/loader.py:232` (`aliases=tuple(colors)`). Probe A/G output: `id=283 label='lamp' aliases=('black','brown','yellow') lev->pillow={'yellow': 2}`; `lev('tap','cup')=2`; `id=74 label='bag'` in the `by_label("bed")` result.
- **Attack scenario:** any scene containing yellow-coloured objects plus a pillow question; any 3–4-letter noun (cup, bed, tv, box — "box" is a training OR noun). Note this also fires at eval time with real perception, because the tracker sets `aliases` from `NOUN_ALIASES` and future caption tokens.
- **Proposed fix (design):** (1) aliases participate in *exact* synonym matching only, never the typo tier; (2) scale the typo budget with length: no fuzzy match for query or candidate < 5 chars, distance ≤ 1 for 5–7, ≤ 2 above; (3) GT loader should carry colours in `caption` only (it already does), not `aliases`.
- **Cost:** S.

## F4 — colour-attribute vocabulary drift: question adjectives can never match VLA-3D's 15-colour scheme names, so the attribute filter always relaxes

- **Severity:** ~0.2–0.4 pts, plus it is the *ignition* for F1 on colour-qualified questions. loft: "black pillows" — 0 of 11 pillows have `black` anywhere (colours are gray/maroon/olive); home_building_2: "red pillows" — the reds are annotated `maroon`. `_attrs_match` (substring over label+caption+aliases) therefore empties the pool, `relax_attributes` fires, and F1 counts all pillows.
- **Evidence:** probe C/D colour dumps; `toolbox.py:148-153` (`_attrs_match`), `toolbox.py:462-466` (relax rung), `loader.py:155-179` (caption = colour scheme names + size token). VLA-3D's closed 15-colour vocabulary is documented in `docs/prior_art/vla_3d.md` / `docs/vla3d_notes.md:176-178`.
- **Attack scenario:** "How many black/red/white X …" in a hidden scene. 2 of 15 training numericals are colour-qualified; the 2025 question generator uses colour disambiguators heavily, so hidden scenes likely include one.
- **Proposed fix (design):** a colour-synonym bridge (mirror of `vocab_bridge`) mapping question colour words onto scheme-name neighbourhoods (red→{red, maroon, crimson}, black→{black, dark gray?} — calibrate the black/gray boundary against the annotated `target_colors` fields, which record what the generator itself called each object). Independently of the bridge, per F1, a relaxed attribute must zero the count, not widen it. Note the *eval-time* variant of this problem is detector-caption colour naming — the bridge belongs in `_attrs_match`, not the GT loader, so it helps both.
- **Cost:** S/M.

## F5 — nested anchor qualifiers are silently dropped by the parser, and definite "the" anchors are evaluated existentially over ALL same-class anchors

- **Severity:** ~0.3–0.6 pts. 4/15 training numericals carry a nested anchor constraint (`docs/question_analysis.md:218`). Probe: "monitors on the table **closest to the map wall decal**" parses to `('on', ['table'])` — the superlative sub-clause vanishes; count = monitors on *any* table. office_1 got lucky (probe E: all 6 monitors sit on table 53, which *is* the decal-closest table); home_building_1 was not lucky ("the sofa **under the pictures**" → any of 4 sofas). Separately, `_eval_clause` is explicitly existential over anchor instances (`toolbox.py:376-381` docstring: "the definite/indefinite distinction is a counting concern, not a filter concern here") — but nothing downstream ever applies it: "on **the** sofa" in a 5-sofa loft counts pillows on all five.
- **Evidence:** probe parse lines for home_building_1 / office_1 / livingroom_1; `toolbox.py:358-366` (`_resolve_anchor` docstring admits it "ignores disambiguator nesting"); `regex_tier.py:131-133` notes indefinite articles but the note is advisory only.
- **Attack scenario:** "How many X are on the Y closest to the Z?" with multiple Ys where X-instances are split across them — the count doubles.
- **Proposed fix (design):** make `Anchor` carry its own clauses and have `_resolve_anchor` recursively resolve them (the machinery exists — it is `resolve()` itself); for definite singular anchors with multiple survivors, pick the salient one (the one that satisfies the most sub-constraints, else nearest/largest) instead of existential-any. This is the same resolver work `question_analysis.md:267` already flags as high-value for OR/IF, so the cost amortizes.
- **Cost:** M.

## F6 — the 15–27% "independent agreement" metric is itself unreliable: the reference counts are inflated, deflated, and category-confused

- **Severity:** 0 eval pts directly — but it corrupts every decision made from the battery, including the sweep (F7). Treating it as pipeline accuracy *over*-states the counting problem on some rows and *under*-states it on others.
- **Evidence:** (i) inflation by loose class matching: `_anchor_agrees` (`scoring.py:134-157`) accepts substring/shared-token matches, so "photo frame" targets count as "photo" — probe: livingroom_3 has **9** photo objects but indep=**10** distinct "photo" targets; the "tv" token alone matches a "tv cabinet" anchor to any TV relation. (ii) deflation by coverage gaps: statements only exist where the generator produced a disambiguating sentence — chinese_room indep=1 vs scene-graph=6=pipeline (report line 25); japanese_room and studio have **zero** annotated targets of the queried class (probe: `annotated ... targets: 0`) yet the report's topline still counts sg-class-only rows as disagreements. (iii) category confusion: `*_class_only` columns are relation-agnostic totals — arabic_room "pipeline=1 vs indep=3(class_only)" (report line 20) compares "sofas below a window" against "all sofas"; scored as disagreement, but the pipeline answer is plausibly correct. The topline "15% over 13" (report line 11) mixes all three.
- **Attack scenario:** not an eval loss — a *calibration* loss: any process (human or sweep) that pushes counts toward these references chases noise.
- **Proposed fix (design):** strict class equality (`normalize_label` + the bridged whitelist only, no substring/token fallback) for target-class matching in `_independent_count`/`_scene_graph_count`; exclude `*_class_only` rows from any agreement statistic (report them as "no independent evidence"); add an annotation-coverage column (annotated targets of class vs CSV instances of class) so deflated rows are visibly untrustworthy.
- **Cost:** S.

## F7 — the cvsweep rerun, as specified, optimizes the wrong counting objective — hold it until F1–F4/F6 land

- **Severity:** indirect but real: adopted thresholds feed OR/IF too (48 of 51 pts). Three independent reasons the numerical term of the sweep cannot do what the brief hopes ("confirm the 11-vs-9 over-count improves", `docs/cvsweep_rerun_brief.md:54-55`):
  1. **Wrong target.** `_best_independent` (`cvsweep.py:244-255`) scores exact agreement with the referential/scene-graph counts *including the class-only variants* ("Class-only variants are still independent evidence ... used when no relation-aware number exists"). Per F6 these are relation-agnostic or inflated: the sweep is literally rewarded for answering "3" to "How many sofas are below a window?" because 3 is the scene's sofa total.
  2. **Flat objective where it matters.** The swept `geometry.on_vert_tol` range is `[0.10, 0.15, 0.20, 0.30]` (`cvsweep.py:161`) while the real pillow-on-sofa gaps are −0.20…−0.58 m (and −1.08 m for the hotel bed headboard): at *every* swept value the on-filter still empties for most pillow questions, the F1 fallback still fires, and the numerical term barely moves — the sweep cannot see the dominant defect, let alone fix it. If the range were widened until pillows passed (~0.6–1.1 m), "on" would degrade into "near" and damage the OR/IF anchoring that carries 16× the points.
  3. **Inert parameter.** `counting.min_obs` `[1,2,3]` cannot change anything: every GT instance has `n_obs=3` (`loader.py:226`) and the scorer itself documents the gate as "inert here by construction" (`scoring.py:361-363`). One of the two counting-labelled sweep dimensions is dead weight.
  The structural fixes (F1, F3, F5) are not threshold-shaped — no point in the sweep space represents "don't fall back to category_only" or "don't typo-match aliases".
- **Evidence:** file:line cites above; `docs/cvsweep_rerun_brief.md:20-22` (objective spec: "independent count agreement ×1").
- **Attack scenario:** the sweep adopts a smaller `on_min_overlap_frac`/larger `near_floor` because it nudged two artifact rows into agreement; the adopted calibration then shifts OR anchor selection on the hidden scenes.
- **Proposed fix (design):** sequence change, not code: land F1+F3 (S), F6 (S), ideally F2/F4, re-run the GT battery, *then* sweep — with class-only rows excluded from the numerical term and `counting.min_obs` dropped from the space (replace with the F2 `on`-geometry params once redesigned). The brief's stability-table adoption rule is good and should stay.
- **Cost:** S (ordering + two small scorer/space edits).

## F8 — the (b) hypothesis is untested, not refuted: eval-time fusion/dedup counting risks the battery cannot see

- **Severity:** unmeasured; plausibly ~0.5–1 pt at eval if unaddressed. The battery's index is CSV-perfect (one record per object, `n_obs=3`, no merge path executed — `gt_battery.py:234`), so nothing in the 15–27% number tests: (i) **no track deletion/decay** — `BasicSceneIndex` never removes an instance, and the head counts at `min_obs=1` (`heads/numerical.py:62`), so a single spurious fusion (ghost cluster passing the 5-point gate, `fusion.py:45`) inflates the count *permanently*; the `n_obs≥3` stability gate only delays the early answer until the watchdog fires anyway. (ii) **association gate vs. object spacing** — `TrackerConfig.gate = 0.75 m` (`tracker.py:79`) exceeds the centre spacing of adjacent same-class small objects (pillows on one sofa are ~0.3–0.6 m apart): greedy matching can cross-associate neighbouring pillows (under-count via merge) or, viewed from a new vantage with centroid shift > gate, mint duplicates (over-count); `MERGE_IOU=0.3` (`scene_index.py:21`) on partial-view clusters is easy to miss. (iii) **count-stability early fire** — 3 consecutive equal counts (`numerical.py:32`) is satisfiable at partial scene coverage (both sofas not yet visited), locking in an under-count if the FSM answers early.
- **Evidence:** file:line cites above; absence-of-execution argument for the battery.
- **Attack scenario:** eval scene, 5 pillows on two sofas; robot sees sofa 1 (3 pillows) for 3+ keyframes → count stable at 3, `winner_margin=0.30` clears the FSM gate → answers 3; true 5.
- **Proposed fix (design):** (i) add track hygiene: decay/prune instances with `n_obs==1` not re-observed within k keyframes; (ii) make the counting min_obs actually filter at answer time (count only `n_obs≥2` instances once any instance has `n_obs≥3`); (iii) gate the early answer on coverage (fraction of frontier exhausted / all rooms visited) for NUMERICAL questions, not just count stability — the watchdog floor already exists to catch the timeout path. Validate in the Phase-2 sim per the playbook (this is the perception-tunables sweep the brief correctly defers).
- **Cost:** M.

## What I could not verify

- **True answers.** Ground-truth integers do not ship in `questions.json` (`docs/question_analysis.md:193`); the per-scene `questions.pdf` answer images were not parsed. All "true count" statements above are triangulated from the object CSVs + scene-graph edges + annotated statements; the wrong-in-7–9/15 estimate and per-finding point allocations inherit that uncertainty (arabic_room, livingroom_1, livingroom_3, studio remain genuinely ambiguous to me — livingroom_3's "photos on the TV cabinet" may be a real *under*-count of ours or annotation over-generation; I could not settle it without the answer key).
- **Whether the challenge's hidden-scene questions use VLA-3D scheme colour names or free colour words** — determines how much F4 matters at eval vs. battery-only.
- **Anything about real-perception counting behaviour (F8)** — no sim available on Windows; all F8 mechanisms are code-level arguments, not measurements.
- **The studio scene-graph anomaly** (report: sg=1 class-only for "framed records" vs 3 CSV objects) — I confirmed the CSV side (3 framed records, 0 annotated referential targets) but did not trace why the scene-graph count collapsed to 1.
- **`between`/`closest` counting behaviour** — no training numerical exercises `between`; the fallback ladder's `drop_relation` rung (which needs ≥2 hard clauses) never fired in the probes, so its counting semantics are untested here, though the same F1 design fix covers it.
