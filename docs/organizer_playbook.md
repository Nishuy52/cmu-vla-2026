# CMU VLA Challenge 2026 — Organizer Playbook

*Compiled 10 Jul 2026*

Research distillation of the two most recent CMU MSR theses produced by the challenge's organizing lab (Zhang Lab / AirLab, advisors Ji Zhang and Wenshan Wang), plus whatever could be found on 2025 challenge winners. Intent: read the organizers' own stated architecture, failure modes, and future-work sections as a signal for what the 2026 challenge will stress, and read what a top-3 2025 team actually built.

---

## TL;DR — Actionable Takeaways

1. **The organizers' reference perception pipeline is: open-vocabulary 2D detection + instance-level 3D tracking → per-object 2D VLM captioning → LLM-based relevance filtering → a hand-written spatial-relation toolbox, not an LLM doing geometry.** This is SORT3D, built by the same lab that runs the challenge and trained/evaluated directly on the VLA-3D dataset the challenge uses for question generation. Building something architecturally similar (or at least respecting the same division of labor between "LLM reasons sequentially" and "code computes geometry") is a safe, organizer-validated starting point.
2. **Never let the LLM compute spatial relations directly.** Zhang's thesis explicitly documents this failure mode: asked for "the nightstand to the left of the bed," an LLM given raw coordinates just picks the smallest x-value object. SORT3D's core fix is a toolbox of ~10 heuristic functions (`find_near`, `find_left`, `find_between`, `order_smallest_to_largest`, etc.) that the LLM calls via chain-of-thought instead of reasoning about coordinates itself. Budget for writing this toolbox — it's cheap engineering with an outsized accuracy payoff (SORT3D goes from 54.5%→60.5% just by adding captions, and the toolbox is what makes view-dependent statements tractable at all).
3. **2D captions on cropped object images measurably help 3D grounding, especially on view-dependent and "hard" (high-distractor) statements** — up to +11.6% on view-dependent Nr3D statements in the ablation. Cheap to add: pick the highest-CLIP-similarity view of each tracked object, caption it with a VLM (Qwen2-VL-7B in their setup, a quantized 3B variant for onboard deployment), template the caption to color/material/shape/affordance.
4. **Object grounding should have an explicit "not found" branch.** Both theses independently flag this: real queries may reference nonexistent objects or contain attribute errors, and models that don't handle it will hallucinate a wrong bounding box rather than reporting failure — costly under the challenge's per-question, no-retry scoring. A simple existence-check (their graph-search baseline hits 90.4% TP / 98.9% TN) is worth including even in a minimal form, since it converts unanswerable/ambiguous test questions from "guaranteed wrong" to "possibly correct or gracefully wrong."
5. **A CMU MRSD team (CopyPasta) placed 3rd worldwide in 2025 using Gemini 2.5 Pro for language/spatial reasoning plus a ROS state machine for explore/map/respond coordination** — i.e., a big general-purpose multimodal model rather than a from-scratch grounding pipeline, wrapped in fairly conventional state-machine autonomy logic. This is a materially different bet than SORT3D's modular-toolbox approach and worth weighing against it: less engineering, more inference cost/latency risk, and less interpretability when it fails.

## Part A — The Organizers' Theses

### A.1 Haochen Zhang, *"Object-Centric Grounding for Deployable and Interactive Vision-Language Navigation Agents"* (CMU-RI-TR-25-86, Sept 2025)

[PDF](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/app/uploads/2025/09/HaochenZhang_MSR_Thesis.pdf) · Advisors: Wenshan Wang & Ji Zhang (co-chairs); committee: Yonatan Bisk, Bowen Li

This thesis is the more directly relevant of the two — it contains a section explicitly titled "Application to CMU VLA Challenge" and its lead author's collaborators (Nader Zantout, Pujith Kachana) are named throughout as co-developers of the challenge's underlying datasets and grounding pipeline.

**Three contributions, in order:**

**1. IRef-VLA benchmark** (Ch. 2). A 3D referential-grounding dataset assembled from five existing scan sources (ScanNet, Matterport3D, HM3D, 3RScan, ARKitScenes) plus custom Unity scenes — **7,635 scenes, 11.5K+ regions, 286K objects across 477 classes, 7.6M heuristically-generated spatial relations, 4.7M referential statements**. Eight relation types are computed exhaustively over object pairs/triples: above/below, closest/farthest, between, near, in, on. Statements are generated to be view-independent, unambiguous, and *minimal* (Grice's maxim of manner — use the fewest descriptors that disambiguate). Critically, IRef-VLA also injects **"imperfect" statements** — referring to objects that don't exist, by mutating an attribute of a real statement — to define a new task: *referential grounding with imperfect references*, requiring the model to (a) detect non-existence and (b) propose a valid alternative.

- Baseline results (training MVT and 3D-VisTA on IRef-VLA, zero-shot transfer to Sr3D/Nr3D): 3D-VisTA trained on full IRef-VLA reaches **65.8% Sr3D / 44.9% Nr3D / 70.8% IRef-VLA-ScanNet accuracy**, outperforming the same model trained on SceneVerse by 1.8% on Sr3D — evidence that better statement heuristics beat just adding more scenes.
- Existence-detection + alternative-generation baseline (LLM-parsed graph search over the scene graph, using gpt-4o-mini): **94% LLM parsing accuracy, 90.4% true-positive / 98.9% true-negative rate on object existence, 61% average alternative-similarity score.**
- **Limitation acknowledged directly:** synthetic language lacks natural view-dependent/allocentric phrasing; the heuristic alternative-scoring metric doesn't capture real human preference.
- **Application to CMU VLA Challenge (§2.8, verbatim purpose statement):** "The release of the IRef-VLA dataset has also been used to support the CMU Vision-Language-Autonomy (VLA) Challenge as a training and question-generation resource... The natural language questions are separated into three categories: numerical, object reference, and instruction following. All queries contain an object-centric spatial reasoning component that requires semantic spatial understanding of the objects in the scene." This is the clearest possible confirmation that challenge questions are generated using the same relation/statement heuristics documented in Ch. 2 — meaning the relation vocabulary (above, below, closest, farthest, between, near, in, on) and the "minimal disambiguating description" style are a solid prior for what test-scene questions will look like.

**2. SORT3D** (Ch. 3) — "Spatial Object-centric Reasoning Toolbox for 3D Grounding using LLMs." This is the system architecture most worth copying from. Four-stage pipeline:

1. **Instance-level semantic mapping** (real-world only; ground-truth boxes used for benchmark evaluation). Open-vocabulary 2D object detection + instance segmentation on camera frames → project registered LiDAR points onto semantic image → per-frame instance point clouds → associate across frames via a 2D tracking module + 3D proximity priors → filtered 3D instance point clouds.
2. **2D captioning per object.** For each tracked object, select the crop with highest CLIP similarity to its label, caption with **Qwen2-VL-7B** (quantized **Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct-3B** for onboard deployment under memory constraints) using a fixed template: *"Describe the \<object\> using properties like color, material, shape, affordances..."* → *"The \<object\> is \<color\>, \<material\>, \<shape\>."*
3. **Relevance filtering.** Two-pass LLM query (**Mistral Large 2**) over the object list: first extract nouns/modifiers from the instruction, then return matching object IDs — narrows hundreds of scene objects down before spatial reasoning, "mimicking human attention."
4. **Spatial reasoning toolbox + parsing.** The filtered object list (`{id, name, caption, cx, cy, cz, size}` per object, coordinates discretized) is handed to an LLM (**GPT-4o** or **Mistral**) with **one in-context example** of chain-of-thought tool use. The LLM decomposes the query into calls against ~10 heuristic functions (Table 3.1: `find_near`, `find_between`, `find_above`, `find_below`, `order_bottom_to_top`, `order_smallest_to_largest`, `find_objects_near_room_corner` [view-independent]; `find_left`, `find_right`, `order_left_to_right` [view-dependent]) and picks a final object ID from the returned candidate lists. For deployment, the LLM instead emits `go_near(target)` / `go_between(target1, target2)` calls that become waypoints for a downstream obstacle-avoidant planner.

- **Results** (200 sampled test statements per split, multiple trials reported with std-dev — notably, the thesis flags that competing LLM-based methods usually *don't* report variance): on Nr3D, SORT3D (GPT-4o) scores **62.0±1.2% overall / 56.6±0.0% view-dependent / 64.3±1.7% view-independent** — competitive with fully supervised SOTA (64.9% SceneVerse-GPS) and the fine-tuned zero-shot SOTA Transcrib3D (65.6%), despite using **zero training data and a single in-context example**. On Sr3D, SORT3D (92.0%) beats all supervised baselines outright. On IRef-VLA's "Hard" split (high-distractor-count statements), SORT3D (GPT-4o) hits **75.0±3.5%**, beating Transcrib3D (GPT-4o)'s 57.5% by a wide margin — the thesis attributes this directly to the 2D captioning module supplying color/size attributes that distinguish near-identical distractor objects.
- **Captioning ablation:** removing captions drops SORT3D from 60.5%→54.5% overall on Nr3D, with the biggest single-category hit on view-dependent statements (56.6%→50.7%, an 11.6-point swing) — the strongest quantified argument in either thesis for investing in a captioning stage.
- **Real-world validation:** deployed on two ground platforms (mecanum-wheel robot, differential-drive wheelchair-base robot), each with a 360° camera + LiDAR (Livox / Velodyne) + onboard NUC + RTX 4090 (16–24GB VRAM). Tested in two unseen environments (student lounge, university corridor) on compound instructions like *"Go to the table next to the bookshelf, then to the chair next to the plant."* Both grounded successfully in the reported trials.
- **Documented failure modes** (Fig. 3.4): (a) logic failures where the model satisfies one spatial constraint but drops another (picks the desk closest to a window, ignores "near the whiteboard"); (b) **pragmatic failures** — picks the literal rightmost pillow instead of inferring the sentence means "a pillow on the bed." These read as compositional-constraint and implicature failures respectively — worth building explicit test cases for.
- **Stated limitations:** hard dependency on internet/cloud LLM APIs (explicitly flagged as a deployment risk, with local-model substitution proposed as the fix); evaluation set size capped at 200 statements/split purely due to LLM inference cost; general shortage of benchmarks for *online* referential grounding under real-time constraints.
- **Future work (§3.9):** extending SORT3D to **outdoor/autonomous-driving** navigation (parking, lane changes) via swapping the semantic mapping backend and spatial toolbox functions — modularity is the explicit selling point. Not directly challenge-relevant but underscores that the toolbox design is intended to generalize by function-swapping, a useful design pattern to imitate.

**3. Interactive / dialogue-based ambiguity resolution** (Ch. 4) — explicitly framed as future work, not a built system. Formalizes ambiguity via information-theoretic entropy over a posterior distribution across candidate target objects, and defines expected information gain of a clarifying question to select the optimal question to ask. Provides a **taxonomy of ambiguity types** (Table 4.1): Vision-Language Misalignment (referential underspecification, missing object/attribute), Agent-User Misalignment (spatial/relational vagueness, subjective terms like "nicer"), Language Ambiguity (syntactic, object co-reference). No dataset or system was built — this chapter proposes a dataset-creation pipeline and a conceptual dialogue-module architecture as future work only.

- **Inference:** since the CMU VLA Challenge protocol (per the GitHub repo, see Part B) does not support back-and-forth dialogue with a human judge — it's a one-shot ROS topic exchange per question within a 10-minute window — this chapter's ideas are not directly implementable in-competition. But the **taxonomy of ambiguity types is still useful defensively**: it's a checklist for what kinds of test questions might be deliberately ambiguous or ill-posed, and Zhang's own recommendation (detect + report uncertainty rather than guess) maps onto the "existence detection" mechanism from Ch. 2, which *is* competition-legal.

**Overall thesis conclusion / cross-cutting future work:** long-term memory, online adaptation to new environments, and reliable onboard (not cloud-dependent) real-time deployment are named as the open problems blocking real-world VLN deployment — a fair proxy for what the organizing lab considers unsolved and worth pushing on.

### A.2 Pujith Kachana, *"Advancing 3D Semantic and Geometric Reasoning"* (CMU-RI-TR-25-18, May 2025)

[PDF](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/app/uploads/2025/05/Advancing_3D_Semantic_and_Geometric_Reasoning__Thesis_-2.pdf) · Advisors: Ji Zhang & Wenshan Wang (chairs); committee: Shubham Tulsiani, Brian Yang. Kachana is a direct collaborator with Zhang (both acknowledge each other and Nader Zantout as co-developers of the same SORT3D/IRef-VLA line of work) — this thesis is best read as a broader-scope companion to Zhang's, covering the *dataset origin* (VLA-3D, the dataset the official challenge repo actually ships) and adding a geometric-reasoning half (visual-LiDAR odometry, 3D foundation models) that Zhang's thesis doesn't cover.

**Structure:** Ch. 3 covers 3D *semantic* reasoning (VLA-3D/IRef-VLA, SORT3D, 2D-feature augmentation of 3D data); Ch. 4 covers 3D *geometric* reasoning (camera-model effects on visual odometry, VOLNet, a brief 3D foundation-model prototype).

**VLA-3D vs. IRef-VLA — important distinction for anyone citing the "official" challenge dataset:** VLA-3D is the *larger, earlier* dataset — same five scan sources plus Unity scenes, but **23.5M spatial relations and 9.7M referential statements** (vs. IRef-VLA's 7.6M/4.7M). IRef-VLA is a later, narrower extension of VLA-3D specifically adding imperfect/ambiguous statements and the existence-detection task. **The official CMU-VLA-Challenge GitHub repo (see Part B) ships VLA-3D, not IRef-VLA**, as its training/question-generation resource — so VLA-3D's plain relation vocabulary (no deliberately-broken statements) is the more direct proxy for actual challenge question style, while IRef-VLA's imperfect-statement design is closer to what organizers might add if they want to stress-test the "graceful failure" behavior described above.

**SORT3D coverage** in this thesis is materially the same system as Zhang's Ch. 3, described here as one of several examples of "adapting pretrained models for 3D tasks without explicit 3D training." Reported numbers are consistent with Zhang's thesis (Nr3D 60.5–62.0% overall, Sr3D ~92%), confirming these are stable, cross-validated results rather than a one-off run.

**2D-feature augmentation of 3D point clouds (§3.4)** — a technique not covered in Zhang's thesis at all, and potentially useful: instead of standard XYZRGB point clouds, augment each point with the **first 8 principal components of AM-RADIO** (a 1152-dim pixel-level vision-language feature model unifying DINOv2/CLIP/SigLIP-style embeddings) → XYZ + 8-dim semantic feature per point. Trained into 3D-VisTA from scratch (no pretraining) and evaluated across five benchmarks:
- Nr3D/Sr3D grounding: **+9.2 points overall on Nr3D** (36.4%→45.6%) vs. the same model without features; smaller but consistent gains on Sr3D, ScanRefer, Scan2Cap, ScanQA, SQA3D.
- **Inference:** this is a heavier-weight alternative to SORT3D's "caption each object separately" approach — instead of captioning per-object crops, it embeds dense per-pixel language-aligned features directly into the point cloud before any object segmentation happens. Given the challenge's 10-minute-per-scene budget and the note that SORT3D already gets a strong return from lighter-weight per-object captioning, this dense-feature approach reads as a research contribution more suited to offline benchmark improvement than a real-time competition pipeline — flagged as a lower-priority option unless per-object captioning proves to be a bottleneck.

**Geometric reasoning (Ch. 4) — directly relevant to the challenge's actual sensor rig:**
- **Camera-model finding:** the thesis explicitly tests whether TartanVO-style monocular visual odometry generalizes from pinhole to **fisheye** images (i.e., wide-FOV cameras structurally similar to the challenge's 360° camera). Result: fisheye VO **fails to generalize** — training loss keeps dropping but validation loss/pose error plateaus or worsens, attributed to fisheye's extra distortion degrees of freedom making the 2D→3D inverse mapping more ambiguous. **Inference:** this is a direct, non-hypothetical warning that reasoning naively about geometry from the challenge's 360° camera stream (e.g., trying to estimate depth or object scale from image position without the LiDAR) is likely to be unreliable — lean on `/registered_scan` LiDAR data for geometry, use the camera stream for semantics/captions only, mirroring exactly how SORT3D itself is architected (LiDAR for 3D boxes, camera crops for captions).
- **VOLNet** — a learned visual-LiDAR odometry model (RGB optical-flow features + LiDAR pointmap features, fused via cross-attention ViT decoder) trained on TartanAir-v2 and zero-shot evaluated on KITTI, beating most prior multimodal odometry baselines on 3 of 4 KITTI test sequences. Not directly reusable (challenge already provides `/state_estimation` at 100-200Hz from the base autonomy stack), but confirms the lab's broader thesis that **fusing camera + LiDAR outperforms either alone** for geometric tasks — consistent with SORT3D's design choice.
- **3D foundation model prototype** — a DUSt3R/MASt3R/VGGT-style model taking unordered images → 3D reconstruction + camera poses, trained on unspecified "internal multi-view data." Described only qualitatively (no benchmark numbers) — a genuinely open research direction, not something available as a citable tool today.

**Conclusion / future work (Ch. 5):** frames the whole thesis as building toward **3D foundation models that unify semantic and geometric reasoning** — i.e., a single model that both understands "what" and "where/how it's shaped," rather than the current two-track approach (LLM-based semantic grounding + separate geometric SLAM/odometry stack). No concrete near-term deliverable is promised; this reads as a long-horizon research agenda rather than a signal about 2026 challenge specifics.

## What This Changes for Our Design

- **Adopt the organizer's division of labor as a baseline architecture**: open-vocabulary 2D detection + LiDAR fusion for 3D object boxes → per-object 2D VLM captioning (color/material/shape/affordance) → LLM-based candidate filtering → a hand-coded spatial-relation toolbox invoked by chain-of-thought → LLM picks/returns final object ID or waypoint sequence. This is the single most validated pattern available: built and evaluated by the people setting the questions, using the same dataset (VLA-3D) that generates them.
- **Write the spatial-relation toolbox first, and don't skip it.** The organizers' own ablation shows LLMs fail at raw-coordinate spatial reasoning (picking min-x for "left of") without it. At minimum implement: near, between, above/below, left/right (view-dependent, computed from free-space-to-target/anchor signed angle, not assumed global axes), smallest/largest-first ordering, closest/farthest-to-anchor. This maps directly onto VLA-3D's eight relation types.
- **Add per-object captioning even if minimal.** The measured accuracy gain (+11.6 points on view-dependent statements) is large relative to the implementation cost (one VLM call per tracked object, using the highest-CLIP-similarity crop). If onboard compute is tight, the thesis's own fallback — a quantized 3B-parameter VLM — is a validated compromise.
- **Build an explicit "object not found" / low-confidence path**, even simple. Given one-shot, no-retry scoring, a wrong bounding box costs the same as "no answer," but a system that can flag uncertainty at least avoids compounding a bad detection into a bad *navigation* action (e.g., driving toward a hallucinated object). This also hedges against deliberately unanswerable or ambiguous test questions, which the organizers' own ambiguity taxonomy suggests they think about.
- **Decide explicitly between "big general model" (CopyPasta's Gemini 2.5 Pro + state machine) and "modular toolbox" (SORT3D) as the core bet**, rather than drifting into a hybrid without deciding. SORT3D is slower to build but interpretable, cheaper at inference, and directly validated on the challenge's own dataset by the people who wrote the questions. CopyPasta's approach is faster to stand up and leans on frontier-model spatial reasoning that the organizers' own thesis explicitly says is unreliable without tool support — the fact that it still placed 3rd suggests strong models plus decent state-machine engineering is competitive, but we have no visibility into how close it came to 1st/2nd, or whether it would have scored higher with a toolbox layered in.
- **Treat the camera stream as semantics-only, LiDAR as geometry-only.** Kachana's fisheye visual-odometry failure is a direct warning against trying to infer depth/scale/position from the 360° camera image alone; use `/registered_scan` for anything positional and the camera purely for VLM captioning/detection, exactly as SORT3D does.
- **Plan an explicit, time-boxed exploration policy** — this is the one piece neither thesis actually solves (SORT3D's real-world demos pre-map the environment manually before querying, which the competition's 10-minute autonomous format does not allow). The independent 2025 arxiv report's frontier-based exploration with a hard time cutoff (500s in their case, scaled to our 10-minute/question budget) is the only concretely described exploration strategy found across both organizer material and third-party reports, and is a reasonable starting point in the absence of anything better-validated.
- **Watch for Yonatan Bisk's IROS 2026 talk** (Pittsburgh, Oct 1, 2026) — he's on Zhang's thesis committee and a confirmed 2026 workshop speaker, making his talk the most likely single source of forward-looking signal about what the 2026 challenge will emphasize, if it's accessible before or shortly after our competition dates.
