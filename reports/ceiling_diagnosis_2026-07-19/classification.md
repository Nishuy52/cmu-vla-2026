# GT-ceiling miss classification (>0.8 m) — 2026-07-19

Classifies every leg from `core.runner.gt_leg_ceiling` (`_if_rubric_geometry` on `main`, as of this diagnosis run) where the resolved leg goal's `dist_goal_to_gt_traj_m` exceeds the 0.8 m arrival tolerance (`core.groundtruth.scoring.LEG_ARRIVAL_TOL_M`). Method, script, and full per-leg fields: `classification.json` (machine-readable) in this directory, generated by a fresh, read-only re-run of `_if_rubric_geometry` + `resolve()`/`idx.by_label()` against `data/vla3d/Unity` — **not** a re-read of the stale `reports/issue59_leg_ceiling.json` (which reports 38/72 and predates later `core/geometry/toolbox.py` commits; a fresh regeneration on current `main` gives **35/72 = 0.4861**, 3 fewer reached legs and 37 misses, not 34).

## Method

For every miss: resolve the leg's anchor instance (or both anchors for `corridor_between`) exactly as `_if_rubric_geometry` does, then compute the minimum distance from the anchor's **own AABB** (not the pushed/offset rubric goal) to the GT reference trajectory (same aligned-frame polyline the ceiling check uses), and search `idx.by_label(noun)` for same-class alternative instances, each scored the same way. Decision rule applied per leg:

1. **(a) WRONG INSTANCE** — a same-class alternative instance's AABB is within 0.8 m of the GT path (or, failing that, within 1.5 m and strictly closer than the chosen one) — the resolver had a better candidate available and picked the wrong one.
2. **(b) OFFSET CONVENTION** — no better same-class alternative exists, but the *chosen* anchor's own AABB is within 1.5 m of the GT path (i.e. a plausible target) while the projected rubric goal is not — a stop-point/goal-projection mismatch.
3. **(c) DEMONSTRATOR ROUTE** — neither the chosen anchor nor any same-class alternative has an AABB within 1.5 m of the GT path anywhere in the scene — the demonstration genuinely did not visit this leg's target class within tolerance.
4. **(d) LEG DECOMPOSITION** — `corridor_between` legs specifically: the leg goal is the straight-line midpoint of a gate between two anchors; classified (d) when at least one anchor's AABB *is* near the GT path (≤ 1.5 m) but the midpoint itself is not — the gate-midpoint arrival definition is structurally wrong for this leg, not a wrong pick or bad projection. Corridor legs with neither anchor near the path fall through to (c) instead.

`NEAR_TOL = 1.5 m` is a diagnosis-only threshold (not a scoring constant) marking "plausibly the right area of the room" vs. "nowhere near"; the scoring tolerance itself stays 0.8 m throughout.

### Mechanism detail per rule change

**B1 — Fix _nearest_free_goal push direction/target (goal-projection convention)** (10 legs)

Anchor AABB is already within the 0.8 m arrival tolerance of the GT path, but the rubric's projected stop point (nearest-free-goal push off the anchor's own or a neighbouring obstacle's footprint edge, clearance 0.4 m, bounded 2.5 m push) lands the goal OUTSIDE tolerance. The push is blind to the GT path/approach direction -- it always moves to the nearest AABB edge, which is frequently the wrong side relative to where the demonstrator actually stood. Fix: bias the push toward the side of the anchor nearest the incoming leg's prior goal (or, cheaper, clamp push distance harder / prefer the raw centroid over a push that increases distance to any reachable point) so the projected stop point never moves further from the anchor's own reachable neighbourhood than the raw centroid did.

**B2 — Widen/soften the arrival-tolerance convention for near-miss stop points** (7 legs)

Anchor AABB itself sits 0.8-1.5 m from the GT path (not touching it), and the projected goal is not appreciably closer -- these are not push-direction bugs, they are a systematic 'the demonstrator stopped near but not AT the object, at a distance the current stop-point convention (project onto/off the AABB edge) does not reach.' Needs a convention change: project the goal toward the GT-path side of the room / a fixed standoff distance from the anchor along the approach direction, not just off the nearest colliding obstacle.

**A1 — Fix superlative-anchor (closest_to/farthest_from) resolution** (5 legs)

The clause's own reference anchor (e.g. 'the hookah', 'the easel', 'the chair') resolves ambiguously when the scene has multiple candidates for it, or the closest_to/farthest_from Euclidean ranking of survivors disagrees with which candidate the demonstrator actually treated as satisfying the superlative -- picking the wrong same-class instance outright. Needs per-case tracing of the reference-anchor resolution (is 'hookah' itself picking the wrong instance) before a single global fix can be specified; likely several distinct root causes bucketed under one symptom.

**A2 — Fix ON/WITH relation-clause fallback ranking** (5 legs)

A residual instance of the same on()/with() hard-gate-vs-soft-score gap documented for issue #59 (7-leg bucket, since partly fixed): the relation clause still fails as a HARD filter for the correct candidate (footprint/height edge cases) and the category-only fallback's soft ranking (_relaxed_relation_order) still does not consistently prefer the geometrically-correct candidate. Needs auditing the specific on()/with() failures per leg (chinese_room tea table, home_building_1 coffee table, home_building_2 magazine-on-ottoman, japanese_room table-with-vase, studio vase-on-cabinet) to find the next edge case the #59 fix didn't cover.

**D1 — Redefine corridor_between leg arrival point (not the raw gate midpoint)** (2 legs)

Both of a corridor leg's anchors can sit right on the GT path (AABB dist 0.0 m and ~1.4-1.8 m) while the straight-line MIDPOINT between them is 1.7-1.9 m off the path -- the demonstrator threaded the gap at an angle, not through the segment midpoint. Fix: score corridor_between legs by whether the GT path crosses the gate SEGMENT (or a widened gate band) anywhere, not by distance to its midpoint; or move the rubric goal to the path's actual closest crossing point on the segment.

**A3 — Fix bare-noun category tie-break (no relation clause at all)** (1 legs)

livingroom_3's 'the cabinet' (no disambiguating clause in this leg) resolves via plain tier-priority/instance-id tie-break among multiple same-class cabinets; the correct one (a TV cabinet, per the scene's role in the sentence) is not the top-ranked survivor. A single-leg symptom; not enough evidence here to prescribe a general rule beyond 'when multiple bare-noun survivors exist, prefer the one closest to elsewhere already established in the route' as a candidate heuristic.

## Caveats

- The (a)/(b)/(c)/(d) split is geometric/automated, not hand-labeled per leg; borderline cases (e.g. an anchor at exactly 1.5 m, or a same-class candidate at 0.81 m) are decided by the thresholds above and stated in each row's `rule_fix` field in the JSON. Spot-checked a sample by hand during this diagnosis (arabic_room stool, chinese_room tea table, livingroom_1/studio corridor legs) — all consistent with the automated label.
- Predicted lifts are additive upper bounds assuming a fix for one rule change class does not regress any currently-reached leg and closes every leg in its bucket to exactly the tolerance — the actual gain from implementing any one rule change should be re-measured against `core.runner.gt_leg_ceiling` after the change, not assumed.
- A1 and A2 are themselves umbrella buckets covering several distinct root causes (different superlative anchors / different on()-with() edge cases) that happen to share a symptom; each may need its own sub-fix rather than one shared patch.
- This diagnosis only classifies MISSES (>0.8 m). It does not re-examine the 35 currently-reached legs, so it cannot rule out fixes here regressing some of them.
